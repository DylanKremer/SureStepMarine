Frequently Asked Questions

Marine decking, explained.

Answers to the questions we hear most about EVA/PE marine decking, care, durability, customization, installation, and the project process.

Decking & performance

What to expect.

Every boat and use case is different, but these are the general guidelines we use when discussing a project.

Why choose EVA/PE decking instead of traditional non-skid?

Traditional molded or painted non-skid is durable and functional, but EVA/PE decking adds benefits that many boat owners prefer: a softer feel underfoot, added traction, reduced deck noise, insulation from the hard deck surface, and far more freedom in color, pattern, borders, rulers, logos, and custom artwork.

It also allows individual panels to be designed around your boat rather than changing the underlying fiberglass or aluminum deck.

Is the decking UV resistant?

The material is intended for outdoor marine use and is designed to tolerate regular sun exposure. Like any exterior material, however, long-term UV exposure, heat, weather, color choice, storage conditions, and maintenance can affect appearance over time.

Darker colors generally absorb more heat than lighter colors, so color selection can matter on boats that spend long periods uncovered in direct sun.

Is it easy to clean?

Yes. Routine cleaning is usually straightforward. For normal dirt, salt, fish residue, and everyday grime:

  1. Rinse loose debris away with fresh water.
  2. Use mild boat soap or a material-safe cleaner.
  3. Agitate stubborn areas with a soft or medium-bristle brush.
  4. Rinse thoroughly and allow the surface to dry.

Avoid aggressive solvents, petroleum-based chemicals, metal brushes, and concentrated high-pressure spray held close to the foam or routed edges. Test unfamiliar cleaners in a small area first.

How long does EVA/PE marine decking last?

There is not one fixed life expectancy for every installation. Service life depends heavily on sun exposure, foot traffic, fishing use, storage, cleaning habits, color, installation quality, and whether the boat is covered when not in use.

With proper installation and normal care, the goal is years of useful service. High-wear areas can age faster than protected areas, and individual damaged panels can often be replaced without redoing the entire boat.

What warranty comes with the decking?

Warranty coverage can depend on the material, project scope, and whether Sure Step Marine performs the installation. We provide the current applicable warranty terms with the project information before the job is finalized rather than publishing a blanket warranty that may not apply to every installation.

If you are comparing a specific project, contact us and we will confirm exactly what is covered.

Can you add custom logos, names, rulers, or artwork?

Yes. Customization is one of the biggest advantages of layered marine decking. CNC routing can expose the contrasting layer beneath the top surface to create logos, boat names, fish rulers, borders, lettering, coordinates, and other artwork.

Custom details are laid out in CAD and included in the approval drawing so you can review placement and scale before the material is machined.

What are your lead times?

Lead times vary with season, project size, templating requirements, design complexity, material availability, and how quickly the final design is approved. We provide a current project-specific estimate when quoting the job.

Custom logos, specialty designs, or material combinations that must be ordered can add time, so early color and design decisions help keep the project moving.

Is the surface slippery when wet?

EVA/PE decking is used in part because of the traction it provides compared with a hard smooth deck surface. No marine surface should be treated as slip-proof, particularly when contaminated with oil, fish residue, soap, sunscreen, or other slick substances, so normal deck safety still applies.

Does the decking get hot in direct sun?

Any exposed deck surface can heat up in direct sunlight. Darker colors usually absorb more heat, while lighter colors generally stay cooler. If surface temperature is a major concern, we can factor that into the color combination before material is ordered.

Can a damaged panel be replaced?

Often, yes. Because the deck is built as individual fitted panels, a damaged section can frequently be reproduced and replaced. Availability of the original material color and the amount of weathering on surrounding panels can affect how closely a new piece matches an older installation.

How do you get the panels to fit my boat?

We digitally template the areas being covered, build the panels in CAD, and provide a construction-style approval drawing before production. Once approved, the panels are CNC machined to the final geometry and design.

This workflow lets us account for seams, borders, hatches, hardware, clearances, pattern direction, and custom details before material is cut.

What thicknesses are available?

Our current material options include 6mm, 9mm, and 13mm constructions. Standard decking commonly uses 6mm material, while thicker configurations can be used for specialty applications, additional layered colors, helm pads, seat cushions, backrests, and coaming bolsters.

Can I see the colors before committing?

Yes. Online colors are useful for narrowing choices, but screens do not reproduce foam colors perfectly. Final material combinations are confirmed with physical samples before material is ordered.

Can existing old foam decking be replaced?

In many cases, yes. The old material and adhesive must be removed and the underlying surface evaluated and properly prepared before new decking is installed. The amount of prep depends on the condition of the existing installation and the substrate beneath it.
